## 1. Business Problem

Madrid is one of the largest short-term rental markets in Europe. The Inside Airbnb snapshot of September 2026 contains about 23,100 active listings in the city, 70% of which are entire homes or apartments. The market is also highly professionalised: around 73% of listings belong to hosts who manage more than one property. In this context, setting the right nightly price is a key business decision. A price that is too high leaves the property empty, while a price that is too low means losing revenue on every booking.

Despite its importance, pricing is still largely done by intuition. Most hosts, especially those with a single property, set their price by browsing similar listings in their neighbourhood. This approach is slow and subjective, and it ignores how much each characteristic of a property (location, size, amenities, host reputation, booking rules) actually contributes to its value.

Existing solutions only partially address this problem. Airbnb's own Smart Pricing tool suggests prices automatically, but it is a black box and it is designed to maximise the number of bookings, which benefits the platform rather than necessarily the host's revenue. Commercial dynamic-pricing tools such as PriceLabs, Beyond or Wheelhouse are more sophisticated, but they charge a monthly fee per listing, are aimed at professional managers and do not explain which factors drive the recommended price.

This project aims to build a transparent, data-driven model that estimates the nightly price of an Airbnb listing in Madrid from its characteristics. Such a model can help new hosts set a competitive initial price, help investors estimate the potential revenue of a property before buying it, and give public authorities an objective reference to analyse the short-term rental market in the city, which is currently under strong regulatory scrutiny.
